NAB steps in over a Rs10 billion timber pile left rotting in Arandu for 20 years

The National Accountability Bureau has written to the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa forest secretary asking for urgent action on a huge stock of illegally felled timber lying in the Arandu Gol forests of Lower Chitral and in Upper Kohistan.

Around 1.4 million cubic feet of wood has been sitting there unsold for more than two decades. The longer it stays in the open, the more it rots, and the easier it becomes for it to quietly disappear. Reports say the trees were originally cut down by militants.

The provincial forest department puts the value of the stock at more than Rs10 billion. Its spokesperson, Latifur Rehman, says a plan to extract and sell the wood was once sent to the provincial cabinet but never got final approval, and that illegal tree cutting remains banned across the province.

NAB now wants a clear provincial policy to dispose of the timber quickly, along with a report on what has been done so far. The bureau is also running an inquiry into forest department officials and staff.

For people in Arandu, the forests are part of everyday life. Many will be asking the obvious question: why did it take twenty years and an anti-corruption agency to move a pile of wood worth billions?
