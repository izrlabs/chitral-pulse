Travel Chitral

Practical guides to the places people ask about most.

Photo: Wikimedia Commons

Kalash Valleys

Bumburet · Rumbur · Birir~1.5 hr from Chitral

Home of the Kalash people, with wooden villages, walnut trees and festivals open to respectful visitors. Plan around Chilam Joshi in mid-May, Uchal in August or Chawmos in December. Always ask before taking photos.

Guesthouses · partnerFestival tours · partner
Photo: Wikimedia Commons

Shandur Pass

~3,700 mPolo festival: early July

Known as the highest polo ground in the world, where Chitral and Gilgit teams face off each summer. Nights are cold even in July, so bring warm layers. Camping spots and tents book out fast during festival week.

Camping packages · partner
Photo: Wikimedia Commons

Tirich Mir

7,708 mHighest in the Hindu Kush

Visible from Chitral town on clear days. Summit expeditions need permits and a licensed operator, but trekkers can reach viewpoints and base areas through the Tirich valley with a local guide.

Trekking operators · partner
Photo: Wikimedia Commons

Chitral Town

Fort · Shahi MosquePolo ground

Start here: walk the riverside Chitral Fort and the white Shahi Mosque, then try local food in the bazaar. It is the base for every valley trip and has the most hotels and transport options.

Hotels in town · partner
Photo: Wikimedia Commons

Garam Chashma

Hot springs~45 km from town

Natural hot springs in the Lutkoh valley, popular for a relaxing day trip, especially in spring and autumn. Trout from the local streams is the meal to order.

Day trip · partner
Photo: Wikimedia Commons

Chitral Gol National Park

Markhor habitatBest: winter mornings

One of the best places to see the markhor, Pakistan's national animal. Winter pushes the herds lower, so sightings are easier from November to March with a ranger or guide.

Wildlife guide · partner

Chitrali food to try

XholayiStuffed bread

Bread baked with a spiced meat filling and cut into wedges, made for guests and celebrations.

Khesta ShapikThick bread

A thick, golden Chitrali bread cooked slowly over the fire.

BratOven-baked bread

A big round bread baked in the traditional clay oven.

RishikiThin crepes

Soft, paper-thin bread that is folded and stacked, often eaten with butter or tea.

ChapotiFlatbread

The everyday round flatbread, cooked on an iron griddle over a wood fire.
